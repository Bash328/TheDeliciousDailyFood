title: "Onigiri (Japanese Rice Balls)"
date: 2026-11-25
category: Lunch
cuisine: "Japanese"
description: "Lightly salted rice balls with a salmon or tuna-mayo filling, wrapped in nori. Good warm or packed for lunch."
image: ""
recipeContent:
  prepTimeMinutes: 30
  cookTimeMinutes: 30
  servingsNumber: 9
  servingsUnit: Onigiri
  ingredients:
    - { amount: "2 1/4 cups", item: "uncooked Japanese short-grain rice" }
    - { amount: "2 1/2 cups", item: "water" }
    - { amount: "As needed", item: "kosher salt" }
    - { amount: "1-2", item: "salmon fillets" }
    - { amount: "1 (5 oz) can", item: "tuna, drained" }
    - { amount: "2 tbsp", item: "Japanese mayonnaise" }
    - { amount: "1 1/2 tbsp", item: "soy sauce, divided" }
    - { amount: "1 packet", item: "bonito flakes (katsuobushi, optional)" }
    - { amount: "3 sheets", item: "nori, cut into thirds" }
    - { amount: "Optional", item: "furikake, for coating" }
  steps:
    - "Rinse the rice until the water runs nearly clear, soak 20-30 minutes, then cook it with the water in a rice cooker or pot."
    - "Salmon filling: salt both sides of the salmon and bake at 220°C / 425°F for 10-20 minutes, until well done and dry. Flake it."
    - "Tuna mayo filling: mix the tuna with the mayonnaise and 1/2 tbsp soy sauce. For a third filling, moisten the bonito flakes with 1 tbsp soy sauce."
    - "Let the rice cool just enough to handle; it should still be warm. Wet your hands and rub a little salt over your palms."
    - "Take about 1/3 cup rice, make a dip in the middle, add 1-2 tsp filling and cover with another 1/3 cup rice. Press gently into a triangle, turning and pressing a few times. Hold it together without packing it tight."
    - "Wrap with a strip of nori, or roll in furikake. If you like the nori crisp, wrap just before eating. You can also shape them in plastic wrap to pack for lunch."
note: "Adapted from Just One Cookbook's onigiri (4.76 stars from 239 votes). Salting your hands seasons the rice and helps it keep."
