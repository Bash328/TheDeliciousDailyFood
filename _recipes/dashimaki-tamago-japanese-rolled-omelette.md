recipeContent:
  prepTimeMinutes: 5
  cookTimeMinutes: 10
  servingsNumber: 2
  servingsUnit: Servings
  ingredients:
    - { amount: "3", item: "large eggs" }
    - { amount: "3 tbsp", item: "dashi (Japanese soup stock)" }
    - { amount: "2 tsp", item: "sugar" }
    - { amount: "1 tsp", item: "soy sauce" }
    - { amount: "1 tsp", item: "mirin" }
    - { amount: "2 pinches", item: "kosher salt" }
    - { amount: "2 tbsp", item: "neutral oil, for cooking" }
  steps:
    - "Crack the eggs into a bowl and whisk by cutting through them with chopsticks in a zig-zag motion, without overmixing."
    - "Combine the dashi, sugar, soy sauce, mirin and salt and mix into the eggs. Pour into a measuring cup with a spout."
    - "Heat a tamagoyaki pan over medium heat and wipe it with a folded paper towel dipped in oil. A drop of egg should sizzle."
    - "Pour in a thin layer of egg and tilt to coat the pan. Pop any bubbles with chopsticks."
    - "When the bottom is set but the top is still runny, roll the egg toward the handle. Push it to the far side, re-oil the pan, pour a second thin layer under and around the roll, and roll again when set but still soft on top."
    - "Repeat until all the egg is used. Lightly brown the outside if you like."
    - "Roll the hot omelette in a bamboo sushi mat to shape and rest 5 minutes. Slice into 1/2-inch pieces and serve with grated daikon and soy sauce if desired."
note: "Adapted from Just One Cookbook's dashimaki tamago (4.64 stars from 288 votes). The dashi makes it very soft and the hardest to roll. A round pan works too, with a shorter roll."
